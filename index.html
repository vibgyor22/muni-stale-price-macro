Muni ETFs price Treasury moves with a two-to-four-day lag

MUB and VTEB absorb about 70% of a Treasury move on the day and the remainder over the following two to four sessions. The strategy trades that residual: long the muni ETF against beta-weighted IEF after Treasury rallies, the reverse after selloffs, entered at the next open.

Mechanism

Most municipal bonds trade a few times a month. Fund NAVs and ETF fair values are built from vendor evaluated prices, which update from sparse trades and Treasury-curve models with a lag. Muni ETF market makers quote around those values, so a Treasury move is only partly reflected in the ETF on the day. The holder base, mainly tax-motivated retail and mutual funds, is slow to rebalance, which also makes fund flows persistent.

  1. Treasuries reprice. IEF moves on macro news.
  2. Muni ETF quotes anchor to vendor evaluated prices.
  3. Same day: about 70% of the eventual move is priced (MUB 73%, VTEB 65%).
  4. Days 1-4: marks catch up as sparse muni trades print. This is the P&L source.
  5. Strategy: measure the Treasury move at the close, take a hedged position in MUB and VTEB against IEF, execute at the next open and hold through the catch-up.

Signal construction

  1. Treasury catch-up (main input). Exponentially weighted IEF return over the last few sessions, scaled by its trailing volatility.
  2. Equity catch-up. The same measure on SPY at 0.3 weight; credit and risk sentiment also reach munis late.
  3. Flow persistence. 60-day momentum in the hedged muni return at 0.5 weight; muni fund flows trend.
  4. Reinvestment calendar. A 0.5 long tilt over the first five business days of each month, when coupon and redemption cash is reinvested.
  5. Execution. Signal at the close, trade at the next open. Each ETF is hedged with IEF at its rolling one-year beta. Costs of 1bp per side on the muni ETF and 0.5bp on IEF. A no-trade band limits turnover to about 60 position units a year.
positiont = clip( 1.0·Tt + 0.3·Et + 0.5·Ft + 0.5·Mt, ±2.5 ),   updated only if the change exceeds 0.75

T, E: EWMA (1-day half-life) of IEF and SPY returns over trailing 60-day volatility. F: 60-day sum of hedged muni returns over its volatility. M: 1 in the first five business days of a month, else 0. The position is held in MUB and VTEB, each hedged with IEF at its rolling 250-day beta.

The specification was selected from 32 variants on 2008-2017 data only. Across all 32, the median 2018-2026 Sharpe ratio is 1.73.

Limitations

  • Capacity. MUB and VTEB trade about $650m a day combined. At 10% volatility the strategy runs 2-3x gross notional, which points to capacity in the tens to low hundreds of millions.
  • Execution. Fills assume the official opening print and fixed costs per side; opening auctions can be noisier in stressed markets.
  • Fat tails. Daily returns have skew of 7.7 and very high kurtosis: the ten best days (led by March 2020 and November 2016, two muni dislocations) make up 19% of total P&L. Removing them leaves the daily Sharpe ratio at 1.84, because those days also carry much of the volatility.
  • Structure. Wider adoption of intraday ETF arbitrage against Treasury futures would compress the lag.

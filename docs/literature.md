# Literature behind each sleeve

## The unifying idea: slow-moving capital and limited attention

| Paper | Finding | Where it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| Duffie (2010), *Journal of Finance*, "Asset price dynamics with slow-moving capital" | When capital cannot move instantly, prices overshoot and then recover over days or weeks. | Every sleeve |
| Mitchell, Pedersen & Pulvino (2007), *AER P&P*, "Slow moving capital" | Specialist markets (convertibles, merger arb) recover slowly from shocks because capital arrives slowly. | Munis |
| Hong & Stein (1999), *Journal of Finance*, gradual information diffusion | News diffuses slowly across investor groups, causing under-reaction and momentum. | Muni catch-up and flow momentum |
| Hirshleifer & Teoh (2003); DellaVigna & Pollet (2009) | Investors under-react to information that is quiet or arrives next to louder news. | Gas storage, crude inventories |

## Munis

| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| Choi, Kronlund & Oh (2022), *Journal of Financial Economics*, "Sitting bucks: Stale pricing in fixed income funds" | Bond fund NAVs (muni funds included) are stale because they use evaluated prices; investors can exploit it through fund flows. Our sleeve finds the same slowness in exchange-traded muni ETF prices relative to Treasuries, and it can be traded in the open market. |
| MSRB, "Price Evaluations" | Explains how vendor evaluated pricing for munis works and why it lags trading. |
| Research on muni ETF price/NAV gaps in March 2020 (Bond Buyer coverage) | Muni ETF prices and NAVs can diverge sharply under stress; the ETF moved first, NAVs caught up. |
| Market lore: "June-July reinvestment season" | Coupon and redemption cash concentrates at the start of months and in summer. We use only the start-of-month calendar. |
